# Where New Drugs could go

September 25, 2026. A roadmap study from the current MVP, the founder's instructions, read-only review of Wayfinder/Pangaea and current primary sources. These are proposed directions and experiments, not implementation commitments.

Separate [Codex-authored unconfirmed proposals](codex-proposals/README.md) were added at the founder's request on October 1. They have no founder approval or implementation authorization and are not the active roadmap.

## Historical study position: v0.9.2

The study below is a possibility map, not a list of still-missing features. Since it was written, production has gained:

- Starter credit allocated at signup, with a shared keyed IPv4/IPv6-network claim limit. Eligible guests still see the available dollar. Onboarding counts saved accounts, excluding the viewer and marked test fixtures.
- Separate operator CLI credentials, report reads/review decisions, confirmation, idempotency and audit. This is report review, not a complete suspension/content-removal system. No additional moderation frontend is planned.
- Opt-in push for invitations and DMs, including subscription revocation and exact return links. Physical iPhone delivery still needs a device check.
- Older AI-chat and DM paging, private semantic search of user/agent messages, exact context jumps, and a return-to-latest control. History results render Markdown and handle links independently of the message jump.
- Post photos and website previews, plus the existing public semantic discovery, selected-post lists and direct social controls.

The founder explicitly ruled out email. Account recovery is not the next build. Backup/restore work remains deferred until usage justifies revisiting it. Keep these decisions separate from unfinished product work rather than repeatedly proposing the same foundation list.

### Founder review after the original study

The founder declined the proposed sequence of bookmarks/muting, small plans, saved searches and then circles. Do not treat that sequence as the next build or keep presenting it as the chosen direction.

The only stated possible expansion is **groups for group posting**, and explicitly **not yet**. This is not approval for events, plans, recurring activities, group chat or a broader group-management product. If revisited, start by clarifying the shared-posting experience and its membership/audience rules.

The founder has since identified **Wayfinder-style automations and an agent inbox** as a direction to investigate: useful incoming information with links and an explicit handoff into the single main chat. See [the source-based adaptation proposal](09-automations-and-inbox.md). The founder confirmed the architectural direction: non-primary-chat agents and external delivery into one private inbox. The adaptation study describes the implementation sequence; no runtime changes or live automations have been made as part of the investigation. The alternatives below remain the original study, not commitments.

## Original study recommendation (not selected)

Build toward **a good local internet where an interesting post or person can turn into a small real plan, and a good plan can become something people do again**.

Keep the public conversation valuable on its own. A funny thread, a useful introduction, a video call or a tiny collaboration can be the whole point. A connection that continues elsewhere can still mean the app worked. New Drugs should not become a clinical friendship funnel, and every post should not need an event form attached.

The agent is a strong advantage because the operating layer already exists. Its next useful job is reducing practical friction: finding relevant human material, presenting a useful list, resolving the next step and keeping a plan consistent. More autonomous behavior should be earned through explicit tasks people actually want it to handle.

I would run one small real-community pilot, build one complete planning slice around the repeated obstacle it reveals, and keep a small account/support/cost foundation track alongside it. I would decide whether circles or better feed tools come next based on what people choose to repeat.

## The possible centers of the product

| Direction | The reason someone would return |
| --- | --- |
| **A. A good local internet** | The people, posts, humor and conversations here are interesting |
| **B. Make small plans** | It is easier to find someone and actually do a thing together |
| **C. Small recurring circles** | A handful of people keep doing something worthwhile |
| **D. A useful private social assistant** | It remembers and follows through on what I explicitly asked |
| **E. Neighbors helping neighbors** | A real offer or request connects me with someone useful |
| **F. Make things together** | I can find a collaborator and get a small project started |
| **G. A shared guide to a place** | I can find worthwhile current options and people to try them with |
| **H. An open social service** | I can use my own agent/client and keep control of my data |

These are not mutually exclusive forever. They are different first priorities. Building their union immediately would obscure which part people actually value.

## Three workable first roadmaps

**Plans first, supported by conversation.** Observe the current loop, build a modest plan card with time/place/audience/RSVP/cancellation, test repeat use, then consider circles or one owner-only watch. This is my provisional first choice.

**Conversation first.** Improve browsing, bookmarks and muting; add a following relationship only if it helps; make searches/collections reusable; let the community's behavior choose whether planning, collaboration or help exchanges come next.

**Circles first.** Bring a few existing groups, define membership/history/privacy carefully, make one shared space and recurring activity work, then let circles choose whether to invite newcomers. This is a strong alternative when the founder can reach real groups before there is broad local density.

The detailed roadmaps use a six-to-twelve-week planning envelope for one useful next loop, with explicit exit conditions. Those are conditional planning ranges, not delivery promises.

## The most important constraints

Human profiles remain human-authored. The normal controls must work without paid AI. CLI/MCP and hosted chat use the same actual capabilities. Nearby and All stay distinct choices. Coarse location remains approximate rather than becoming live tracking. The agent needs exact authority for social commitments, and background work needs visible scope, budget and an off switch.

The no-profit commitment also needs an operating model. Paid AI credit, starter allowances, shared search/hosting costs and voluntary support are different things. A useful app still needs someone to handle reports, support and recovery.

The original study identified guest credit allocation, report handling, older chat access and off-app delivery as immediate gaps. Their current status and the founder's decisions are recorded above. Account export/deletion, stronger moderation actions and operational recovery remain longer-term responsibilities; they are not prerequisites being added to the next feature request.

## Original proposed next step (not selected)

Choose the first real cohort and one repeated social intention it already has. Use the existing app enough to find the practical obstacle. Then build **one complete plan flow**, including the unglamorous parts such as changing the time, cancelling and returning to the right conversation.

If the cohort mostly loves posting and replying, let that evidence choose the conversation roadmap instead. The point is to make this particular social space useful and enjoyable, not to force everyone through the same definition of a successful social life.
